I can feel the wind blowing through despite the heat. The mangoes are starting to ripen too. Kang Asep has come all the way from the mountains of Banten to trade his vegetables with our garden’s crop of snake fruits. My daughter is having a blast showing her friend the new pollinator-friendly hybrid flowers we are developing. I’ve asked Koh Aling to make Kang Asep and his daughter fried rice for lunch. I hope we can re-pave the road sooner, the weeds sometimes get stuck on my wheelchair. It’s a beautiful Sunday and I am glad to be alive right now.

## Illustrator Notes:

This piece takes quite a while to finish, partially my fault and partially unexpected drawing tablet blowup. I started gardening too and that’s why I drew the hibiscuses. I love hibiscus flowers.  I wanted to depict a Solarpunk future that is specific to my region, Jakarta, Indonesia, South East Asia. I based this market/urban garden off an area where people hung out a lot. I quite like the way it is set up in a circle.  As you can see, there is a roof that also doubles as a solar panel. In the centre there is a mango tree complete with an explanation plaque with braille and regular letters. As for the food stalls, I want it to be a compilation of my favourite jajan pasar (market snacks). Starting with a fried rice/char kway teow stall, classic Chinese-Indonesian food, savoury, spicy and smoky, followed by es campur, shaved ice with mixed fruits and a hint of syrup, perfect for a hot day, then  towards toko kue, baked goods, serving traditional cakes like kue lapis, but also stuff like croissants and ending with batagor, fried fish cakes in peanut sauce. There is also a grass jelly climbing plant next to the shaved ice stall, because I imagine if you can get it as fresh as you can? Why not?

I specifically focused on a single disabled mom and her daughrer and Kanekes people for a few reasons. We in Jakarta have often failed disabled people - and I want to imagine a future where they can live with dignity. The Kanekes are indigenous people living in the Banten province and they still live off grid. I want to imagine a future where we live - not entirely off grid but adopting some of their ways so the earth can breathe a little easier. I hope I get to see more location specific Solarpunk soon!
