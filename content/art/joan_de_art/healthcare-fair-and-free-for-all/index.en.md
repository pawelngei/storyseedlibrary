Once we had a healthcare system that was expensive, complicated, dysfunctional and completely broken. People suffered from medical errors and died from treatable conditions due to their race and class and gender. Access to seeing a doctor was tied to employment instead of medical need. Disabled communities were denied care by eugenic panels of insurance companies that left people to die in pain. A Western focus on healthcare ignored centuries of indigenous knowledge and Eastern medicines. Focus was spent on specialty silos instead of preventative care like nutrition, mental health, primary care and sexual health and health education. It was a fragmented, broken system that put profits over our people.

Something had to change.

We removed shareholders, debt, for-profit systems and CEO’s from the equation, and ensured a baseline level of care for everyone. Provider burnout and staffing shortages were addressed, and medical school was made free. Patients and their doctors were put in charge instead of insurance companies.

Documentation was simplified and shared across hospitals and clinics and pharmacies, no more fragmented medical history or misdiagnoses. Medications were made free, along with every surgery, treatment, rehab, equipment, therapy, birth and hospice care. It cost us nothing to get well again.

Meal trains and local support systems became intertwined with community care, entire blocks took care of their sick neighbors. Indigenous methods and medicines were taught and practiced by medical professionals, cultural competency brought entire spiritual or family systems back into patient healing.

Environmental hazards and chemicals that caused cancers and illnesses were banned and removed from our land. Invisible illnesses were made visible. Rest was required. Nature was welcomed back into our healing circles with medicine gardens, animal therapy, forest bathing, sunshine, clean air and water.

Communities healed, and so did we.
