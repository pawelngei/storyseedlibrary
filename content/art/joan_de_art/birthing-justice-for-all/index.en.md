Welcome to the world little one, we finally built it for you. 

Once, having children was expensive, isolating and even dangerous. Childcare and housing prices made it difficult to find a safe place to have a baby. Loneliness and post-partum depression skyrocketed as couples or individuals were abandoned by society at their most vulnerable. 

Pregnancy itself was dangerous; not only did we have high maternal and infant mortality rates, but miscarriages and abortions were criminalized and punished. Everything about babies was politicized, pressurized and commodified. Companies offered no paid family leave. The future was terrifying as climate change made it hard to justify having young at all. People without children were shamed and stigmatized as well, there seemed was no way to win. 

You cannot destroy a nest then ask why there are no eggs. 

So we changed it. We began to center children instead of banishing them from sight. It started by creating a physical space for families to rest. Parking lots were deconstructed and revamped into playgrounds, sensory gardens, grief centers, family bathrooms, supply swaps, therapy centers, food pantries, shade trees, benches. 

Elders and teens were brought back into the community fold too. Meal trains ran around the clock, and night-shifts were shared by a loving system of many hands. Lullabies replaced blaring commercials, roaring engines with bicycle bells, concrete with nature. Sleep was returned to all. 

We channeled our energy and resources into community care, gave doula support and medical advocacy, reproductive care and abortion access to anyone who needed it. Grief was acknowledged. Women’s health research was fully funded, and relationship and sex education classes were freely given. Childcare was baked into every system. No longer did someone need to hunt for a place to breastfeed or access hygiene products.

 As the village returned, it became safe and affordable and enjoyable to have children again. No longer were they seen an expensive inconvenience, but beloved protectors of our collective future. 

Welcome home.
