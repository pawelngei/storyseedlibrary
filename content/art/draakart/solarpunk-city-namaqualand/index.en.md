Submission for the 2023 Solarpunk City Art Collaboration.

The bioregion depicted is the Deserts and Xeric Shrublands of Namaqualand, South Africa, a desert famous for its unique ability to change from a dusty wasteland into a very lush wildflower field after the first rains of spring. This is set just after the rains.

The buildings featured are Ndebele-style homes, which are made of cob and straw. They are excellent for the environment of South Africa and trap heat in their walls during the very hot days and release this heat in the frigid nights.

The city itself is connected by raised tramlines that traverse the landscape in a web-like formation. All stations are within walking distance. The large building in this painting is a library of some sort.

Some seasonal crops grown include pearl millet and cassava. The year-round food sources are cattle, poultry, sour fig and groundnut. During dry spells, where other crops fail, there are ample fruits from marula, sourplum and monkey orange.

Drinking water is drawn up from wells using wind turbines, which, alongside solarpanels, are the town's staple energy sources. The one depicted is purely for pumping water, however.
The large dogs in the paintings are Boerbols, which were traditionally used for lion hunting, but now act as guardians, especially to the children. The smell of livestock is bound to attract predators to the settlement, especially during lean times.

The guinea fowl are companion animals to the chooks, acting as sentries.
