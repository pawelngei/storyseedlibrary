The annual Harvest Festival descends from the centuries-old Polish and Kashubian harvest celebrations, held by the end of summer. It is no longer only a celebration of crops, but a place to strengthen resilience and promote mutual aid. The traditional time of Harvest Festival, the end of summer, is also the driest time of the year, with the biggest wildfire impact, so there is a seed exchange event with many of the seeds being saved and used to regenerate the burnt areas.

Farms operate through community-supported partnerships. Residents purchase shares in local farms - sharing both the harvests and the risks of a difficult growing season. If drought or fire destroys crops, members can choose to accept community credits instead of produce, helping farmers to recover. In the illustration, we can see the farmer's booths/stands and people gathered around them. 

Wildfire prevention has become a major community effort. Instead of relying solely on cleared firebreaks ("fuel breaks"), residents have created living resilience corridors ("fuel break matrix") throughout the landscape. Wetlands, ponds, community gardens, orchards, and hugelkultur mounds built from decaying wood help retain water, support biodiversity, and slow the spread of fire. It's visible in the surrounding landscape.

The scars of a recent wildfire are still visible during this year's festival. Burned tree trunks stand on the horizon. Partially charred logs are being repurposed into new hugelkultur beds (hugelkultur - a raised garden mound built from buried logs, branches, and organic matter that stores water and slowly releases nutrients as it decomposes). 

One of the most recognizable groups present in the festival is the Beaver People Guild. Decades earlier, they began as volunteers working to reverse generations of drainage and forest melioration. By filling abandoned drainage ditches, restoring wetlands, and increasing the land's ability to retain water, they helped bring moisture back into the landscape. What began as a grassroots movement has since become a respected civic institution. The beaver has become a symbol of ecological restoration throughout the region. 

Last but not least, there is music present all throughout the festival, which helps people connect and relieve their hardships.
Many activities and actions during the festival radiate around water, because this element is the "protagonist" of this world.
